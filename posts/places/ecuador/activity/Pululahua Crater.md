Pululahua Crater (just north of Quito, near **Mitad del Mundo**) is one of the only inhabited volcanic craters in the world, and the whole area is a **geobotanical reserve**. Visiting it can be as quick as a viewpoint stop or a full-day nature excursion. Here’s a breakdown of what to do there:

---

## 🌄 At the Rim (Summit Area)

- **Mirador de Pululahua (Viewpoint)** – The main overlook gives sweeping views of the massive crater valley (about 4 km across). On a clear day you’ll see farmland, mist swirling in, and distant ridges.
- **Hotel Restaurant El Cráter** – Right at the rim; great for coffee, a meal, and the famous **crater-swing** for dramatic photos.
- **Photography** – The clouds roll in quickly; mornings are usually clearest. Sunset can also be spectacular if the mist cooperates.
    

---

## 🚶 Inside the Crater

- **Hiking & Trails** – Trails descend into the crater where you can walk among fields, forests, and small communities.
    - Popular hikes include routes to the **volcanic domes** inside the crater (like Pondoña Hill).
    - You can do short walks (1–2 hrs) or longer treks guided by locals.
- **Horseback Riding** – Some local residents offer horseback tours through the valley floor.
- **Camping** – Pululahua is a designated **Geobotanical Reserve**, so camping is allowed in certain areas (with prior arrangement). Nights are cool and very quiet.  

---

## 🌱 Nature & Wildlife

- **Unique Microclimate** – Because of the crater’s shape, the valley has its own ecosystem—mist often settles in, making it cooler and wetter than surrounding areas.
- **Birdwatching & Botany** – The reserve has orchids, hummingbirds, butterflies, and a mix of Andean flora.
- **Geology Interest** – You’re standing inside a dormant caldera! The volcanic domes inside are remnants of eruptions thousands of years ago.
    

---

## 🏡 Local Culture
- **Community Farms** – People live and farm inside the crater, growing maize, beans, and flowers.
- **Small Hostels & Eco-lodges** – You can stay overnight for the quiet, misty atmosphere and morning hikes.
    

---

## Schedule

 An inhabited volcanic crater with hiking trails and viewpoints. Checkout the lookout. Detour to the **Pululahua Geobotanical Reserve** for crater views where people still farm inside. 
*Optional:* Picnic with leftover bizcochos + cheese.

 **Tips**
- Go early in the day before the clouds fill the crater.
- Bring layers—it can be chilly and damp compared to Quito.
- If you don’t want to hike down, the rim viewpoints alone are worth the stop (often paired with Mitad del Mundo and Intiñan Museum in a half-day trip).
